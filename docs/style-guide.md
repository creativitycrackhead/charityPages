# Style guide

This site helps local mosques start community programs. Its voice and look borrow from charities known for clear, trusted communication. Use this guide when you add or edit ideas.

## What we learned from successful charities

| Charity | What they do well | What we took |
|---|---|---|
| **charity: water** ([brand book](https://nonprofitf22.classes.andrewheiss.com/files/branding/brandbook-2016.pdf)) | Inspirational, clear and hopeful. "Write like a person, not a company." Say "can", never guarantee. Real people and places. Flat illustration, no gradients or shadows. One bold color plus a bright accent. Proof for every claim (photos, GPS). | Hopeful tone, "can" not "will", flat icons, one brand color and one accent, a link to proof for every example. |
| **National Zakat Foundation** ([nzf.org.uk](https://nzf.org.uk/)) | A single mission line in the hero ("Help build stronger communities through the power of local Zakat"). Impact numbers right below it. A Qur'an verse as a quiet, faith-centred pause. Separate paths for givers and people who need help. | Mission-first hero, a number strip, a Qur'an verse band, and clear next steps. |
| **Sufra NW London** ([about](https://www.sufra-nwlondon.org.uk/?p=126)) | Calls the people it serves "guests". Rooted in the local community. Food aid is "essential, but not the solution". | People-first words: neighbors, families, guests. Programs build dignity and skills, not just handouts. |
| **Islamic Relief USA, Day of Dignity** ([release](https://irusa.org/?p=5080)) | "In a compassionate and dignified manner." Service framed as communities coming together. | "Dignity" as a value. The masjid as a hub for the whole neighborhood. |
| **LaunchGood** | Community-powered giving that celebrates the many people taking part, around moments like Ramadan. | Tie programs to the Islamic calendar (Ramadan, both Eids, back to school). |
| **Splash** and **Water Mission** voice guides ([Splash](https://splash.org/assets/docs/Brand-Messaging-Guidelines-for-External-Use.pdf), [Water Mission](https://brand.watermission.org/verbal-identity/voice-and-tone)) | "Potential, not pity." Name the need, then lead with hope. Invite people as partners. Headlines that pair a need with a benefit ("Because all children deserve..."). Language a 16-year-old understands. | The "Because..." tagline on every idea. Readers are invited as partners, not asked to feel guilty. |
| **Nonprofit design roundups** ([Numiko 2026](https://numiko.com/insights/the-best-non-profit-websites-2026)) | A strong opening, simple navigation, filters by theme, and statistics paired with stories. | A short top menu (Ideas, How to start, About), category filters, and examples next to every idea. |

## Voice

- **Hopeful and practical.** Name the need in one line, then move straight to what a masjid can do.
- **People first.** Write "families", "neighbors", "elders", "guests". Don't write "the needy", "the poor" or "less fortunate".
- **Say "can", not "will".** Never promise an outcome. Never invent numbers. Every figure needs a source or the word "often".
- **Write like a person.** Use contractions and short sentences. Explain any Arabic term the first time it appears on a page where it matters (zakat, qard hasan, jama'ah).
- **Invite, don't pressure.** The reader is a volunteer or masjid leader, and a partner in the work.
- **Respect fiqh.** Give practical suggestions and send rulings to the imam or a scholar.
- **Commas:** no Oxford comma ("rice, lentils and oil"). Avoid "&" in body copy, though it's fine in short titles.

## Writing an idea

1. **Title:** what it is, in plain words ("Masjid pantry and halal food shelf").
2. **Tagline:** "Because..." plus the human reason ("Because hunger doesn't keep office hours.").
3. **Impact:** one sentence on what it can do for a household.
4. **Why it matters:** three bullets.
5. **Proven by:** two or three real organizations with a link, ideally at least one mosque or Muslim-led group.
6. **Steps:** five or six, each marked Simple, Moderate or Complex. Moderate and Complex steps say when to start and when they're done.
7. **Watch out for:** two or three honest risks.

## Look

- **Colors:** deep masjid green (`#14532d` family) as the main color, saffron gold (`#f2b33d`) as the one bright accent, warm cream backgrounds. Each category has a soft tint. Dark mode uses the same hues, lifted.
- **Type:** Fraunces (a warm serif) for headlines, Inter for everything else.
- **Shapes:** flat icons and an eight-pointed star motif from Islamic geometric art. No gradients on icons and no drop shadows on illustrations.
- **Layout:** big headline, generous space, one main button per section.
- **Photos:** none for now. If you add photos, show people with dignity and get consent.
